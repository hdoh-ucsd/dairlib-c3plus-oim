# xArm6/OIM environment validation report

**Date:** 2026-09-05. **Audit branch:** `audit/xarm6-plant-fidelity` (base `0abc0eb7c`, c3plus-channel-route-v1). **Execution build:** post-P5 binaries in `.claude/worktrees/obstacle-lcs-contact` (same build that produced `c3plus_p5_fix_validation`). **Outputs:** `/root/push_anything_ADMM/results/xarm6_environment_validation/` (mirrored under `results/parallel_c3plus_audit/agent_b_xarm6_plant/` on this branch). No cost, route, ranking, C3-weight, or obstacle-formulation changes were made.

## Naming caveat discovered first

Despite the OIM/xArm6 heritage, the OIM-scene demos (`push_t_bt010_{open_table,single_obstacle,shelf_gap}`) load a **Franka** (`panda_arm.urdf`) in Drake via `franka_sim.cc`. The only xArm6-native Drake environment is the separate `examples/sampling_c3/oim_t` open-table gate arc. Everything below validates the environment that actually runs the C3+/OIM experiments.

## Answers to the 13 report questions

1. **Is the intended object loaded correctly?** YES. `push_t_oimscale_m01.sdf`: total mass exactly 0.1000 kg (0.0528504 + 0.0471496), boxes 0.089×0.0198×0.0596 + 0.0198×0.0794×0.0596, inertias per link verified, spawn (0.5, 0.3, 0.0008) with roll=pitch=yaw=0, min object-table signed distance **−1.1e-16 m** (exact rest, not floating, not penetrating), 0.167 m from the nearest obstacle at t=0 (`object_model_comparison.csv`, `initial_scene_state.json`, `initial_scene.png`).
2. **Same object model in the controller?** Dynamically identical. The controller loads `push_t_oimscale_m01_control.sdf` — byte-different but same masses/inertias/COMs/boxes, plus 3 witness spheres r=0.001 by design. **One mismatch:** effective sim friction is μ=1.0 on every pair (the SDFs omit friction → Drake default; the μ=0.3 ground block is commented out), while the planner uses `mu_per_pair_type` [0.4165, 1, 0.4615]. EE-object matches; ee-ground and object-ground do not. Per project memory this μ-literal mismatch is reference-conformant (the reference sim ships it too), so it is flagged, not fixed; it is a plausible contributor to the measured 0.61 in-contact realization gain.
3. **Rendered/collision geometry matches?** YES — 2 collision boxes (sim) / 2+3 witness spheres (controller), dimensions verified against the SDF; top-down render consistent.
4. **Can the arm move the pusher to commanded Cartesian locations?** YES. From the success run's EE trace: ±x and ±y segments all move in the commanded direction (net/path 0.70–0.89, 17–81 mm/s), 9/9 reposition episodes decrease EE-to-target distance, zero sign inversions, zero OSC warnings (`xarm6_cartesian_sanity.csv`).
5. **Can the pusher physically acquire contact?** YES, but the commanded reposition target is a **pre-contact standoff by design**: across 41 reposition completions the EE-object gap at "reached repositioning target" is median 0.0756 m (0.057–0.120). Acquisition happens afterwards and converts at 41% in the success run vs 14–18% in the plateau failures (`contact_acquisition_sanity.csv`).
6. **Once in contact, can it translate the object?** YES — first acquired contact moved the object 28.7 mm within 1 s; scripted exact-contact push: 0.058 m object translation per 0.100 m pusher travel (`object_dynamics_sanity.csv`); live open_table pushes complete a 0.6 m transport.
7. **Can it rotate the object?** YES — off-center push produces the correct yaw sign (−1.017 rad measured); open_table trials solve a 180° yaw goal to 0.003–0.05 rad.
8. **Can it complete open_table?** **YES — 3/3 fresh serial trials succeeded** under flock at the current build: 49.9 s / 131.2 s / 97.4 s, final errors 0.014–0.019 m and 0.031–0.100 rad, 13–35 repositions, zero workspace aborts, zero topples, zero buffer overflows (`open_table_results.csv`).
9. **Can it complete shelf_gap?** YES, demonstrated once in six post-P5 draws at identical settings: 0.018 m / 0.063 rad at t=139.8 s with a single 22.9 mm/s southward corridor burst. The five failures all stall at the corridor mouth with contact re-acquisition failure (contact fraction →0, 36–55 buffer-overflow lines, reposition storms) (`shelf_gap_results.csv`).
10. **What prevents the failures?** Contact acquisition and local planning, not the environment: yaw-misaligned corridor arrival → the sampler/repositioner finds no admissible pushing pose → candidate-pool starvation → indefinite reposition/no-progress cycling. Single_obstacle failures split into genuine C3+ local fixed points (2), one topple (final roll −1.57 rad), and production plateaus; one historical end-to-end success (0.020 m at 70.2 s) proves capability (`single_obstacle_results.csv`). Workspace and stability are non-factors on open_table; timing is clean (all streams monotonic, no gaps >1 s; one benign 2× decimation in the state logger vs its configured 10 Hz) (`timing_sanity.csv`).
11. **Any reason to modify the plant itself?** NO. The only substantive planner-vs-sim gap is the friction-literal mismatch (question 2), which is reference-conformant and must not be changed without explicit authorization. One tooling defect was found and worked around, not in the plant: `scripts/c3ab/record_full_state.py` hardcodes a deleted job-tmp path for its generated LCM python bindings; this audit regenerated minimal decoders (see run_manifest.yaml).

## Verdict

**B. XARM6 ENVIRONMENT IS VALID, BUT CONTROLLER-LAYER FAILURES LIMIT RELIABILITY.**

The object loads exactly as intended and rests exactly on the table; geometry, mass, inertia, pusher representation, Cartesian motion, contact physics, and timing all validate; open_table completes 3/3 and both obstacle scenes have proven end-to-end successes at current or near-current builds. Reliability on the obstacle scenes is limited by contact re-acquisition and local planning (candidate starvation at yaw-misaligned corridor arrival, C3+ local fixed points), which are controller-layer phenomena — consistent with the Agent-B fidelity verdict (B4/B6 dominant). The one environment-adjacent finding, the friction-literal mismatch (planner 0.4165/0.4615 vs sim effective 1.0 on ground pairs), is reference-conformant and documented for a future authorized A/B.
